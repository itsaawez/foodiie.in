Step-by-Step Instructions

6 steps
  1. 1

    Step 1

    In a bowl, mix the paneer, onion, capsicum, tomato, coriander, chaat masala, pepper and salt.

  2. 2

    Step 2

    Spread green chutney on one side of each bread slice.

  3. 3

    Step 3

    Divide the paneer mixture between two slices and cover with the remaining slices, chutney-side down.

  4. 4

    Step 4

    Heat a tawa or sandwich press. Butter the outside of each sandwich.

  5. 5

    Step 5

    Toast 2-3 minutes per side until crisp and golden.

  6. 6

    Step 6

    Cut diagonally and serve hot with extra chutney or ketchup.

💡

Chef's Pro Tip

For optimal flavor, ensure all fresh spices are freshly roasted or ground before adding. Adjust seasoning gradually according to taste and let the dish rest for 3–5 minutes before serving.

Maharashtrian

Veg Kolhapuri

Veg Kolhapuri is a fiery Maharashtrian mixed vegetable curry from Kolhapur, known for its deep red colour, bold spices and thick, clingy masala. Cauliflower, carrots, beans, peas, potato and capsicum are simmered in an onion-tomato base so every piece is well coated rather than swimming in gravy. It tastes hot, savoury and slightly smoky, with soft vegetables and a little bite from the capsicum added towards the end. Serve it hot with bhakri, chapati, phulka or steamed rice for lunch or dinner, and it can fit into a balanced diet when paired with a plain dal or salad on the side. Practical tip: do not overcook the vegetables before they go into the masala — keep them just tender, as they soften further while simmering and can turn mushy if cooked twice for too long.

North Indian

Paneer Tikka

Paneer Tikka is a popular North Indian starter of paneer cubes marinated in spiced hung curd and cooked until the edges char lightly and the centre stays soft. This is a home version made in an oven, on a grill, or on a tawa — a clay tandoor is not required, and the marinade does most of the work. The taste is smoky, tangy, and warmly spiced, with sweet bites of capsicum and onion between the paneer. Serve it hot as a starter or snack, or tuck it into wraps and rolls. The marination takes about 30 minutes and is inactive time, so it is not counted in the prep time below. One practical tip: use hung or very thick curd and pat the paneer dry first — a watery marinade slides off and the tikka steams instead of charring.

35 minutes
