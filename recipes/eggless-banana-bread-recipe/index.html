Eggless Whole Wheat Banana Bread

4.8/5 (0 reviews)
Jump to Recipe
Share: WhatsApp Facebook X Pinterest

This Eggless Whole Wheat Banana Bread is a soft, moist tea-time loaf made with ripe bananas and whole wheat flour (atta), with no egg used anywhere in the recipe. It tastes gently sweet and banana-rich, with a warm hint of cinnamon, a tender crumb and a little crunch from walnuts. Bake it at 180°C for about 45 minutes — the Cook Time in this recipe — and serve it warm or at room temperature with tea or coffee, or pack slices for a snack box. This recipe makes 10 slices. One practical tip: use bananas that are ripe with dark spots on the skin, mash them completely smooth, and do not overmix the batter after adding the flour, or the loaf can turn dense.

Step-by-Step Instructions

9 steps
  1. 1

    Step 1

    Preheat the oven to 180°C. Grease a loaf tin of about 20 cm x 10 cm and line its base with baking paper.

  2. 2

    Step 2

    Peel the bananas and mash them completely in a large bowl with a fork until smooth and lump-free.

  3. 3

    Step 3

    Add the brown sugar, oil, milk and vanilla extract to the mashed bananas and whisk until well combined.

  4. 4

    Step 4

    In a separate bowl, mix the whole wheat flour, baking powder, baking soda, cinnamon powder and salt.

  5. 5

    Step 5

    Add the dry mixture to the banana mixture in two parts, folding gently with a spatula until just combined. Do not overmix.

  6. 6

    Step 6

    Fold in the chopped walnuts, keeping a few pieces aside.

  7. 7

    Step 7

    Pour the batter into the prepared loaf tin, level the top, and sprinkle the walnut pieces kept aside over the top.

  8. 8

    Step 8

    Bake at 180°C for 40–45 minutes, until the top is golden brown and a toothpick inserted in the centre comes out clean.

  9. 9

    Step 9

    Cool the loaf in the tin for 10 minutes, then remove it, cool it on a rack for at least 20 minutes, and cut it into 10 slices before serving.

💡

Chef's Pro Tip

For optimal flavor, ensure all fresh spices are freshly roasted or ground before adding. Adjust seasoning gradually according to taste and let the dish rest for 3–5 minutes before serving.

North Indian

Rice Kheer

Rice Kheer is a slow-cooked Indian rice pudding made by simmering rice in milk until the grains turn soft and the milk thickens into a creamy, lightly sweet dessert. It has a gentle cardamom flavour, a silky texture with soft bites of rice, and a topping of almonds, pistachios and saffron that adds a little crunch. It is served at festivals, family meals and celebrations, and works equally well warm in winter or chilled in summer. Serve it in small bowls, because it is rich and filling. One practical tip: rinse and soak the rice briefly and stir the milk often as it simmers, so the kheer thickens evenly and does not catch at the base of the pan.

North Indian

Malai Kulfi

Malai Kulfi is a dense, creamy frozen dessert made by reducing milk slowly until it thickens, sweetening it, and freezing it in moulds. Unlike light, churned ice cream, kulfi is firm and rich, with a malai-like taste, a gentle cardamom flavour and small pieces of pistachio and almond in every bite. It is a favourite summer treat and a good make-ahead dessert for guests. This recipe makes 8 kulfis. Please note the timing: the Cook Time below is the active milk-reduction time, and the kulfis then need 6–8 hours of inactive freezing time in the freezer before they are ready to unmould and serve. One practical tip: reduce the milk properly and let the mixture cool completely before pouring it into moulds, which helps the kulfi set dense and smooth.

60 minutes

Indian

Coconut (Nariyal) Ladoo

Coconut Ladoo, or Nariyal Ladoo, is a soft, white sweet made from desiccated coconut cooked briefly with condensed milk and cardamom, then rolled into round ladoos. The taste is milky and gently sweet, the texture is soft and slightly chewy, and a light coating of coconut gives each ladoo a neat finish. These are handy for festivals, pooja offerings and last- minute guests because the mixture comes together in one pan and needs no syrup. This recipe makes 12 ladoos. One practical tip: let the mixture cool just enough to handle before rolling, and grease your palms lightly if it sticks — rolling while it is too hot makes loose, flat ladoos.

25 minutes
