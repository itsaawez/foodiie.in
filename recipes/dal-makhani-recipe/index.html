Dal Makhani

4.8/5 (0 reviews)
Jump to Recipe
Share: WhatsApp Facebook X Pinterest

Dal Makhani is a slow-cooked Punjabi dal of whole black urad and rajma simmered with tomato, butter, and cream until the gravy turns silky and the lentils almost melt. The flavour is rich and gently spiced, with a deep, rounded savouriness that only builds with time on low heat. Be clear about the timings before you start: the overnight soaking of the lentils and most of the long, low simmer are inactive time — the dal needs an occasional stir, not constant attention, and the hands-on work is short. That inactive time is counted in the prep and cook figures below, so the total is honest. Serve it with naan, jeera rice, or plain rotis for a weekend lunch or a special dinner. One practical tip: keep the simmer truly low — a hard boil makes the gravy thin and harsh instead of creamy.

Step-by-Step Instructions

9 steps
  1. 1

    Step 1

    Combine the rinsed black urad dal and rajma in a large bowl, cover generously with water from the measured 1.5 litres, and soak for 8 hours or overnight. This soaking is inactive time and is included in the prep time above. Drain before cooking.

  2. 2

    Step 2

    Add the soaked lentils to a pressure cooker with fresh water from the measured quantity, enough to cover them by about 5 cm. Pressure cook on medium heat for 8–10 whistles, until the lentils are completely soft and the rajma can be mashed between your fingers. Let the pressure release naturally.

  3. 3

    Step 3

    Melt the butter in a heavy-bottomed pan on medium heat. Add the onion puree and cook for 8–10 minutes, stirring often, until it turns golden and the raw smell goes.

  4. 4

    Step 4

    Add the ginger paste and garlic paste and fry for 1 minute. Add the tomato puree and Kashmiri red chilli powder and cook for 8–10 minutes, until the mixture thickens and the butter begins to separate at the edges.

  5. 5

    Step 5

    Add the cooked lentils along with their cooking liquid and the salt. Mash a small portion of the lentils against the side of the pan with the back of a ladle to help thicken the gravy.

  6. 6

    Step 6

    Simmer on the lowest heat for 60–75 minutes, stirring every 10–15 minutes and scraping the base, adding a little of the remaining measured water at a time if the dal becomes too thick. This long simmer is mostly inactive time — the dal largely looks after itself between stirs — and it is counted in the cook time above.

  7. 7

    Step 7

    Crush the kasuri methi between your palms and stir it in, along with the garam masala. Simmer for 5 more minutes.

  8. 8

    Step 8

    Stir in the fresh cream, reserving a spoonful for garnishing if you like, and simmer gently for 2 minutes without boiling hard. Switch off the heat.

  9. 9

    Step 9

    Garnish with the fresh coriander leaves and the reserved cream, and serve hot.

💡

Chef's Pro Tip

For optimal flavor, ensure all fresh spices are freshly roasted or ground before adding. Adjust seasoning gradually according to taste and let the dish rest for 3–5 minutes before serving.

South Indian

Medu Vada

Medu Vada is a crisp, golden South Indian fritter of ground urad dal, shaped like a small doughnut, with a fluffy, soft centre and a crunchy crust. The dal is soaked, ground to a thick, airy batter, and given a short rest — most of that preparation time is inactive soaking and resting, not hands-on work, and it is counted in the prep time below so the total stays honest. The active work is the grinding, shaping, and frying, which together take under an hour. Medu vada is a classic tiffin and breakfast item, usually served with sambar and coconut chutney, and it is also festive enough for a weekend spread. One practical tip: grind with as little water as possible, adding it a spoon at a time — a loose batter will not hold its shape and will drink up oil while frying.

Bisi Bele Bath

Bisi Bele Bath is Karnataka's much-loved one-pot meal of rice, toor dal, and vegetables cooked together until soft and porridge-like, then flavoured with tamarind, jaggery, and a warm, aromatic spice powder. The name simply means hot lentil rice, and it should be served steaming hot, loose in consistency, with a spoonful of ghee melting over the top.
