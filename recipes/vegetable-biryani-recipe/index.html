Vegetable Biryani

4.8/5 (0 reviews)
Jump to Recipe
Share: WhatsApp Facebook X Pinterest

Vegetable Biryani is a layered rice dish where fragrant basmati rice is cooked over a spiced vegetable masala made with onion, tomato, curd and whole spices, then sealed and steamed on dum so the layers absorb each other's aroma. The rice should stay separate and fluffy, the vegetables tender but not mushy, and every serving should have both white and masala-coated grains. It suits festive lunches, Sunday family meals and dinner guests, served with raita or a simple salad. A practical tip: cook the rice only until it is about 70 percent done before layering — it finishes cooking in the dum steam, and fully cooked rice turns soft and broken by the end.

Step-by-Step Instructions

11 steps
  1. 1

    Step 1

    Warm the milk and soak the saffron strands in it for at least 15 minutes. Keep aside for layering.

  2. 2

    Step 2

    Boil the water in a large pot with 1 tsp salt, 1 bay leaf, 2 green cardamom pods, 2 cloves and half the cinnamon stick. Add the drained soaked rice and cook for 5 to 6 minutes until the grains are about 70 percent cooked — firm in the centre but not raw. Drain immediately and spread on a plate.

  3. 3

    Step 3

    Heat the oil and 1 tbsp ghee in a heavy, wide pot on medium heat. Add half the sliced onions and fry for 8 to 10 minutes until crisp and golden brown. Remove and keep aside for layering.

  4. 4

    Step 4

    In the same pot, add the remaining bay leaf, cardamom pods, cloves, cinnamon and the cumin seeds. Let the spices crackle for a few seconds.

  5. 5

    Step 5

    Add the remaining sliced onions and cook for 5 to 6 minutes until golden. Add the ginger- garlic paste and slit green chillies and cook for 1 minute.

  6. 6

    Step 6

    Add the chopped tomatoes and the remaining 1 tsp salt. Cook for 4 to 5 minutes until soft.

  7. 7

    Step 7

    Add the mixed vegetables and potato cubes. Mix well, cover and cook on low heat for 6 to 7 minutes until the vegetables are just tender.

  8. 8

    Step 8

    Reduce the heat to low. Add the whisked curd, turmeric powder, red chilli powder and biryani masala powder. Stir continuously for 2 to 3 minutes until the masala thickens slightly and coats the vegetables. Switch off the heat.

  9. 9

    Step 9

    For layering, spread half the vegetable masala in the pot if it is not already in an even layer, then cover with half the rice. Sprinkle half the fried onions, half the mint leaves, half the coriander leaves, half the saffron milk and 1 tbsp ghee over the rice. Repeat with the remaining masala, rice, fried onions, herbs, saffron milk and ghee. Drizzle the lemon juice on top.

  10. 10

    Step 10

    Cover the pot with a tight lid, sealing the edges with a clean kitchen towel if the lid is loose. Cook on high heat for 3 minutes, then on the lowest heat for 20 minutes for the dum. Switch off and rest, covered, for 10 minutes.

  11. 11

    Step 11

    Open the lid, gently fluff and mix only the top layer with a fork, and serve hot, digging down so each portion gets both rice and vegetables.

💡

Chef's Pro Tip

For optimal flavor, ensure all fresh spices are freshly roasted or ground before adding. Adjust seasoning gradually according to taste and let the dish rest for 3–5 minutes before serving.

Punjabi

Chole (Kabuli Chana Curry)

Chole is a Punjabi gravy curry of kabuli chana (chickpeas) simmered in a thick, tangy onion-tomato masala with ginger, green chillies and warming spices. The gravy should coat the chickpeas rather than drown them, and the chickpeas themselves should be soft enough to mash easily between two fingers. It is a favourite for weekend lunches and family dinners, served with bhature, pooris, jeera rice or plain rotis, along with sliced onion and a lemon wedge. The dish is planned ahead because the chickpeas need an overnight soak. A practical tip: reserve the chickpea cooking water and use it for the gravy — it adds body and a deeper colour that plain water cannot give.

North Indian

Dal Tadka

Dal Tadka is a homestyle yellow dal made from arhar (toor) dal pressure-cooked until soft, simmered with a simple onion-tomato base, and finished with a sizzling tadka of ghee, garlic, cumin and dried red chillies poured over the top just before serving. The dal itself is smooth and mildly spiced, while the tadka adds the aroma and flavour that make the dish special. It is an everyday lunch or dinner staple served with steamed rice or rotis, and a little extra ghee on top is very welcome. A practical tip: add the tadka at the very end and cover the dal for 2 minutes — the dal absorbs the smoky garlic flavour much better than if you stir and serve immediately.

35 minutes

North Indian

Baingan Bharta

Baingan Bharta is a smoky, mashed brinjal sabzi made by roasting a large brinjal directly over a flame until the skin chars and the flesh turns soft and fragrant, then cooking it with onion, tomato, ginger, garlic and everyday spices. The texture is soft and slightly chunky, not smooth like a puree, and the flavour is earthy with a gentle heat from green chillies. It is a comforting dinner dish that pairs beautifully with hot rotis, phulkas or even plain dal and rice. Serve it fresh and hot, because it loses its smoky punch on reheating. A practical tip: roast the brinjal until it completely collapses and feels heavy and soft — under-roasted brinjal stays spongy and never mashes well.

45 minutes
