Aloo Paratha

4.8/5 (0 reviews)
Jump to Recipe
Share: WhatsApp Facebook X Pinterest

Aloo Paratha is a Punjabi whole wheat paratha stuffed with a spiced mashed potato filling, rolled out gently and roasted on a tawa with ghee until golden spots appear on both sides. The outside should be crisp at the edges and soft inside, with the potato filling spread evenly right up to the edges — not collected in the centre. It is a much-loved breakfast, though it works just as well for lunch or a travelling meal, served with curd, pickle or a knob of butter. A practical tip: the potato filling must be completely cool and dry before stuffing; warm or wet filling makes the dough tear while rolling and leak on the tawa.

Step-by-Step Instructions

8 steps
  1. 1

    Step 1

    Make the dough: mix the whole wheat flour with half the salt in a large bowl. Add the water gradually and knead for 6 to 7 minutes into a soft, smooth dough. Smear the oil over the surface, cover and rest for 20 minutes.

  2. 2

    Step 2

    Make the filling: mash the boiled potatoes thoroughly so there are no lumps. Mix in the chopped onion, green chillies, grated ginger, red chilli powder, garam masala, amchur, coriander powder, ajwain, chopped coriander leaves and the remaining salt. The filling should be dry and hold together; let it cool completely before using.

  3. 3

    Step 3

    Divide the dough and the filling each into 8 equal portions. Roll each dough portion into a smooth ball.

  4. 4

    Step 4

    Dust a dough ball with flour and roll it into a small disc of about 10 cm. Place one portion of filling in the centre, bring the edges up and together, and pinch to seal like a pouch. Press gently to flatten.

  5. 5

    Step 5

    Dust the stuffed ball lightly with flour and roll gently and evenly into a paratha of about 18 to 20 cm, using light pressure so the filling spreads without tearing the dough. Repeat for the remaining portions.

  6. 6

    Step 6

    Heat a tawa on medium heat. Place a rolled paratha on it and cook for about 1 minute until small bubbles and light spots appear on the surface.

  7. 7

    Step 7

    Flip the paratha, spread about 1/2 tbsp ghee on the cooked side, and cook for 1 minute. Flip again, spread ghee on the second side, and press gently with a spatula, especially at the edges, until both sides have golden brown spots and the paratha puffs slightly.

  8. 8

    Step 8

    Remove to a plate and repeat with the remaining parathas, using the remaining ghee. Serve hot with curd or pickle.

💡

Chef's Pro Tip

For optimal flavor, ensure all fresh spices are freshly roasted or ground before adding. Adjust seasoning gradually according to taste and let the dish rest for 3–5 minutes before serving.

Punjabi

Chole (Kabuli Chana Curry)

Chole is a Punjabi gravy curry of kabuli chana (chickpeas) simmered in a thick, tangy onion-tomato masala with ginger, green chillies and warming spices. The gravy should coat the chickpeas rather than drown them, and the chickpeas themselves should be soft enough to mash easily between two fingers. It is a favourite for weekend lunches and family dinners, served with bhature, pooris, jeera rice or plain rotis, along with sliced onion and a lemon wedge. The dish is planned ahead because the chickpeas need an overnight soak. A practical tip: reserve the chickpea cooking water and use it for the gravy — it adds body and a deeper colour that plain water cannot give.

Kashmiri

Kashmiri Dum Aloo

Kashmiri Dum Aloo is a rich potato curry where small whole potatoes are fried until golden and then simmered slowly in a curd-based gravy scented with fennel, dry ginger, and Kashmiri red chillies. The gravy is deep red, mildly hot, and aromatic rather than fiery, and the potatoes soak up the flavour as they cook on dum. Traditionally, this version is made without onion and garlic, which keeps the taste clean and lets the whole spices come through. Serve it with steamed rice, naan, or rotis for a festive lunch or a special weekend meal. One practical tip: whisk the curd well and add it on low heat, stirring continuously for the first minute — this stops the gravy from splitting.

55 minutes

South Indian

Masala Dosa

Masala Dosa is a crisp, golden crepe made from fermented rice and urad dal batter, folded around a spiced potato filling. The dosa is thin and lacy at the edges, the filling is soft and yellow with turmeric, mustard seeds and curry leaves, and together they make a filling breakfast or a light dinner. Please plan ahead: the rice and dal need about 6 hours of soaking and the batter then needs 8–12 hours to ferment in a warm place — that soaking and fermentation time is inactive time and is not included in the prep and cook times below. A practical tip: spread the batter on a hot but not smoking tawa, and sprinkle a few drops of water to cool the pan slightly between dosas.

60 minutes
