Dhaba-Style Egg Curry

4.8/5 (0 reviews)
Jump to Recipe
Share: WhatsApp Facebook X Pinterest

Dhaba-style egg curry is defined by a well-browned onion-tomato masala, eggs that are fried before they go into the gravy, and a finish of garam masala and kasuri methi. Frying the boiled eggs gives them a light golden skin that holds up in the curry and stops them tasting plainly boiled against a rich gravy. The masala needs patience more than technique: cook the onions until properly golden and the tomatoes until the oil separates, because that browning is the flavour base of the whole dish. Keep the gravy medium-thick so it coats the eggs and works with both roti and rice. Halve one egg at serving if you like, so the yolk shows, and serve the rest whole. Leftovers keep well overnight in the fridge, and the gravy tastes even more settled the next day when reheated gently.

Step-by-Step Instructions

10 steps
  1. 1

    Step 1

    Boil the eggs for 10 minutes, cool them in water, peel, and prick each egg lightly with a fork. Rub them with 1/4 tsp of the turmeric powder and 1/2 tsp of the red chilli powder.

  2. 2

    Step 2

    Heat 1 tbsp oil in a kadai on medium heat. Fry the eggs for 2 to 3 minutes, turning them, until light golden in patches. Remove and set aside.

  3. 3

    Step 3

    Add the remaining 2 tbsp oil to the same kadai. Add the cumin seeds and let them crackle.

  4. 4

    Step 4

    Add the onions and cook for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring often, until deep golden.

  5. 5

    Step 5

    Add the ginger-garlic paste and cook for 1 minute.

  6. 6

    Step 6

    Add the tomatoes, green chillies, the remaining turmeric powder, the remaining red chilli powder, coriander powder and salt. Cook for 6 to 8 minutes until the masala thickens and the oil separates at the sides.

  7. 7

    Step 7

    Pour in 300 ml hot water, stir, and bring to a simmer. Cook for 3 minutes to bring the gravy together.

  8. 8

    Step 8

    Add the fried eggs. Cover and simmer on low heat for 5 minutes so the eggs absorb the flavour of the gravy.

  9. 9

    Step 9

    Sprinkle in the garam masala and kasuri methi, stir gently, and cook for 1 minute.

  10. 10

    Step 10

    Switch off the heat, garnish with coriander leaves, rest for 5 minutes, and serve hot with roti or rice.

💡

Chef's Pro Tip

For optimal flavor, ensure all fresh spices are freshly roasted or ground before adding. Adjust seasoning gradually according to taste and let the dish rest for 3–5 minutes before serving.

Kadai Mushroom

Kadai mushroom is a semi-dry curry built around two things: mushrooms cooked just long enough to stay firm, and a freshly pounded kadai masala of coriander seeds and dried red chillies that tastes noticeably different from a ready garam masala alone. Onion and tomato form the base, capsicum and onion petals go in near the end so they stay crisp, and kasuri methi finishes the dish with the familiar restaurant-style aroma. The main mistake to avoid is crowding wet mushrooms into a cool pan, which makes them steam and turn rubbery. Wipe them clean, cut them large, and add them to a properly hot masala. Serve with roti, naan or jeera rice. Because the gravy is thick rather than soupy, it also packs well for lunch the next day, reheated gently so the mushrooms do not overcook further.

Masala Khichdi

Masala khichdi takes the plain rice-and-dal khichdi most homes know well and gives it a proper tempering, vegetables and warm spices, so it eats like a complete meal rather than a fallback. Rice and yellow moong dal cook together in one pot until soft and just loose, while onion, tomato, carrot, peas and beans add colour and bite. A final ghee tempering with cumin and dried red chilli goes over the top at serving, which is where much of the aroma comes from. It suits weeknight dinners, lunch boxes that will be reheated, and days when a single pot is all the cooking anyone wants to do. Serve it hot with curd, papad or pickle on the side, and adjust the water at the end: khichdi thickens as it stands, so a splash of hot water brings it back to a soft, spoonable consistency without diluting the seasoning if you check the salt afterwards.

Gujarati Kadhi

Gujarati Kadhi is a thin, pale-yellow curry made from curd and besan, gently sweetened and finished with a ghee tempering of mustard seeds, cumin, curry leaves and dry red chillies. Unlike thicker, pakoda-filled kadhis from some other regions, this version is light and pourable, with a clear sweet-sour balance. It is usually served poured over steamed rice, often as part of a simple Gujarati thali at home. The texture should be smooth, never grainy or split. A practical tip: whisk the curd and besan until completely lump-free before the pan goes on the heat, and keep stirring in one direction while it comes to a boil — that is what keeps the kadhi from curdling.

30 minutes
