Sweet Corn Vegetable Soup

4.8/5 (0 reviews)
Jump to Recipe
Share: WhatsApp Facebook X Pinterest

Sweet Corn Vegetable Soup is a light, comforting Indo-Chinese soup filled with sweet corn, carrots, beans, and cabbage in a gently seasoned, slightly thickened broth. It tastes mildly sweet from the corn, with a soft peppery warmth at the end, and the vegetables stay tender with a little bite. It works well as a starter before an Indo-Chinese meal or as a light evening bowl on its own. Serve it piping hot, as the flavour feels flat once it cools. A practical tip: crush a portion of the corn and stir it back into the soup — it adds body and a stronger corn flavour without using extra cornflour.

Step-by-Step Instructions

7 steps
  1. 1

    Step 1

    If using frozen sweet corn kernels, thaw them. Keep 2 tablespoons of kernels aside, lightly crush the remaining corn with the back of a spoon or a brief pulse, and set both portions aside.

  2. 2

    Step 2

    Heat the oil in a deep pan or wok on medium heat. Add the finely chopped ginger and garlic and cook for 30 seconds until fragrant.

  3. 3

    Step 3

    Add the spring onion whites and cook for 1 minute. Add the diced carrot, chopped green beans, and shredded cabbage. Stir-fry for 2–3 minutes so the vegetables soften slightly but retain a bite.

  4. 4

    Step 4

    Pour in the 750 ml water and bring to a boil. Add the crushed sweet corn and the whole kernels kept aside. Simmer for 5–6 minutes.

  5. 5

    Step 5

    Add the soy sauce, vinegar, black pepper powder, and salt. Mix well and taste the soup.

  6. 6

    Step 6

    In a small bowl, mix the cornflour with the 50 ml water to make a smooth slurry. Pour it into the soup in a thin stream, stirring continuously.

  7. 7

    Step 7

    Simmer for 2–3 minutes until the soup thickens slightly and looks glossy. Switch off the heat, garnish with the spring onion greens, and serve hot.

💡

Chef's Pro Tip

For optimal flavor, ensure all fresh spices are freshly roasted or ground before adding. Adjust seasoning gradually according to taste and let the dish rest for 3–5 minutes before serving.

Kachumber Salad

Kachumber Salad is a fresh, crunchy Indian salad of finely chopped onion, tomato, and cucumber tossed with lemon juice, roasted cumin, and coriander. It is tangy, juicy, and lightly spiced, and it adds a clean contrast to rich curries, dal, biryani, and grilled dishes. There is no cooking involved, so it comes together in minutes and is best made just before the meal. Chop the vegetables evenly and not too large, so every spoonful has a mix of all three. A practical tip: add the salt and lemon juice at the very end, as salt draws water from cucumber and tomato and can make the salad soggy if it sits too long.

North Indian

Boondi Raita

Boondi Raita is a cooling side dish of whisked curd mixed with crisp, tiny fried gram flour balls called boondi, seasoned with roasted cumin, black salt, and a pinch of chilli. It is creamy and mildly tangy, with soft boondi in some bites and a light crunch when served fresh. It is most often served with biryani, pulao, parathas, and spicy curries to balance the heat of a meal. This is a no-cook recipe, so the only real work is whisking the curd smooth and seasoning it well. A practical tip: add the boondi just before serving if you like it crisp, or 15 minutes earlier if you prefer it soft.

Punjabi

Paneer Butter Masala

Paneer Butter Masala is a rich, creamy main course of soft paneer cubes simmered in a smooth tomato, butter, and cashew gravy. The taste is mildly sweet, tangy, and gently spiced, with a silky texture that pairs well with naan, roti, or jeera rice. It is a popular choice for weekend lunches and for guests because the gravy can be made ahead and reheated. Add the paneer only in the last few minutes of cooking and avoid boiling it for long, as overcooking makes paneer firm and rubbery. A practical tip: blend the tomato-cashew base until completely smooth and strain it if needed for that classic velvety finish.
