Masala Dosa

4.8/5 (0 reviews)
Jump to Recipe
Share: WhatsApp Facebook X Pinterest

Masala Dosa is a crisp, golden crepe made from fermented rice and urad dal batter, folded around a spiced potato filling. The dosa is thin and lacy at the edges, the filling is soft and yellow with turmeric, mustard seeds and curry leaves, and together they make a filling breakfast or a light dinner. Please plan ahead: the rice and dal need about 6 hours of soaking and the batter then needs 8–12 hours to ferment in a warm place — that soaking and fermentation time is inactive time and is not included in the prep and cook times below. A practical tip: spread the batter on a hot but not smoking tawa, and sprinkle a few drops of water to cool the pan slightly between dosas.

Step-by-Step Instructions

8 steps
  1. 1

    Step 1

    Wash the rice, urad dal and methi seeds, then soak them together in enough of the water to cover well for about 6 hours. Soak the rinsed poha separately for 15 minutes before grinding.

  2. 2

    Step 2

    Drain and grind the soaked rice mixture with the poha, adding the remaining water a little at a time, to a smooth, pourable batter. Mix in half the salt, cover and ferment in a warm place for 8–12 hours, until the batter has risen and smells mildly sour.

  3. 3

    Step 3

    For the filling, crumble the boiled potatoes into rough chunks with your hands, keeping some texture.

  4. 4

    Step 4

    Heat 2 tbsp of the oil in a kadhai on medium heat. Add the mustard seeds and let them splutter, then add the curry leaves, onion, green chillies and ginger. Cook for 3–4 minutes until the onion softens.

  5. 5

    Step 5

    Add the turmeric powder and the remaining salt, then add the potatoes. Mix gently and cook for 3 minutes. Switch off the heat and stir in the lemon juice and coriander leaves.

  6. 6

    Step 6

    Stir the fermented batter gently. Heat a cast-iron tawa on medium-high heat and grease it lightly with a little of the remaining oil.

  7. 7

    Step 7

    Pour a ladleful (about 80 ml) of batter in the centre and spread it quickly in a spiral into a thin circle. Drizzle a few drops of the remaining oil around the edges and cook for 2–3 minutes until crisp and golden underneath — do not flip it.

  8. 8

    Step 8

    Place 2–3 tbsp of potato filling in the centre, fold the dosa over it and serve immediately. Repeat with the remaining batter and filling.

💡

Chef's Pro Tip

For optimal flavor, ensure all fresh spices are freshly roasted or ground before adding. Adjust seasoning gradually according to taste and let the dish rest for 3–5 minutes before serving.

Maharashtrian

Bharli Vangi (Stuffed Brinjal)

Bharli Vangi is a Maharashtrian curry of small brinjals stuffed with a roasted peanut and coconut masala, then simmered until tender in their own spiced gravy. The filling is nutty and warmly spiced with goda masala, with a small touch of jaggery and tamarind balancing the heat. The brinjals turn soft and almost creamy inside while holding their shape in the pan. It is a homestyle main that pairs well with bhakri, chapati or plain steamed rice and dal. A practical tip: keep a little of the stuffing aside to stir into the gravy — it thickens the sauce and makes sure none of the masala is wasted if a brinjal splits while cooking.

Indo-Chinese

Gobi Manchurian

Gobi Manchurian is an Indo-Chinese favourite of crisp fried cauliflower florets tossed in a glossy, garlicky soy-based sauce. The florets should stay crunchy under the sauce, with spring onion greens scattered on top for freshness. It works as a party starter on its own, or as a side with fried rice or plain noodles for a weekend dinner at home. The sauce is savoury, mildly sweet and just spicy enough to keep you reaching for another piece. A practical tip: toss the fried florets in the sauce only at the very end and serve straight away — even five minutes of sitting in the sauce will soften the coating you worked to make crisp.

45 minutes

South Indian

Medu Vada

Medu Vada is a crisp, golden South Indian fritter of ground urad dal, shaped like a small doughnut, with a fluffy, soft centre and a crunchy crust. The dal is soaked, ground to a thick, airy batter, and given a short rest — most of that preparation time is inactive soaking and resting, not hands-on work, and it is counted in the prep time below so the total stays honest. The active work is the grinding, shaping, and frying, which together take under an hour. Medu vada is a classic tiffin and breakfast item, usually served with sambar and coconut chutney, and it is also festive enough for a weekend spread. One practical tip: grind with as little water as possible, adding it a spoon at a time — a loose batter will not hold its shape and will drink up oil while frying.

4 hours 55 minutes
