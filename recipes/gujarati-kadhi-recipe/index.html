Gujarati Kadhi

4.8/5 (0 reviews)
Jump to Recipe
Share: WhatsApp Facebook X Pinterest

Gujarati Kadhi is a thin, pale-yellow curry made from curd and besan, gently sweetened and finished with a ghee tempering of mustard seeds, cumin, curry leaves and dry red chillies. Unlike thicker, pakoda-filled kadhis from some other regions, this version is light and pourable, with a clear sweet-sour balance. It is usually served poured over steamed rice, often as part of a simple Gujarati thali at home. The texture should be smooth, never grainy or split. A practical tip: whisk the curd and besan until completely lump-free before the pan goes on the heat, and keep stirring in one direction while it comes to a boil — that is what keeps the kadhi from curdling.

Step-by-Step Instructions

7 steps
  1. 1

    Step 1

    Whisk the curd and besan together in a large bowl until completely smooth with no lumps. Whisk in the water, a little at a time, to make a thin, pourable mixture.

  2. 2

    Step 2

    Pour the mixture into a deep pan. Add the sugar, salt, grated ginger and the slit green chilli.

  3. 3

    Step 3

    Cook on medium heat for 12–15 minutes, stirring continuously in one direction, until the kadhi comes to a gentle boil and the raw smell of besan goes away. Lower the heat and simmer for 2 more minutes.

  4. 4

    Step 4

    For the tempering, heat the ghee in a small pan on medium heat. Add the mustard seeds and let them splutter.

  5. 5

    Step 5

    Add the cumin seeds, methi seeds, curry leaves, dry red chillies and hing, in that order, and swirl the pan for a few seconds until fragrant.

  6. 6

    Step 6

    Pour the hot tempering straight into the kadhi and cover the pan immediately for 1 minute so the aroma stays in.

  7. 7

    Step 7

    Stir in the coriander leaves and serve hot, poured over steamed rice.

💡

Chef's Pro Tip

For optimal flavor, ensure all fresh spices are freshly roasted or ground before adding. Adjust seasoning gradually according to taste and let the dish rest for 3–5 minutes before serving.

South Indian

Masala Dosa

Masala Dosa is a crisp, golden crepe made from fermented rice and urad dal batter, folded around a spiced potato filling. The dosa is thin and lacy at the edges, the filling is soft and yellow with turmeric, mustard seeds and curry leaves, and together they make a filling breakfast or a light dinner. Please plan ahead: the rice and dal need about 6 hours of soaking and the batter then needs 8–12 hours to ferment in a warm place — that soaking and fermentation time is inactive time and is not included in the prep and cook times below. A practical tip: spread the batter on a hot but not smoking tawa, and sprinkle a few drops of water to cool the pan slightly between dosas.

Maharashtrian

Bharli Vangi (Stuffed Brinjal)

Bharli Vangi is a Maharashtrian curry of small brinjals stuffed with a roasted peanut and coconut masala, then simmered until tender in their own spiced gravy. The filling is nutty and warmly spiced with goda masala, with a small touch of jaggery and tamarind balancing the heat. The brinjals turn soft and almost creamy inside while holding their shape in the pan. It is a homestyle main that pairs well with bhakri, chapati or plain steamed rice and dal. A practical tip: keep a little of the stuffing aside to stir into the gravy — it thickens the sauce and makes sure none of the masala is wasted if a brinjal splits while cooking.

60 minutes

Karnataka

Bisi Bele Bath

Bisi Bele Bath is Karnataka's much-loved one-pot meal of rice, toor dal, and vegetables cooked together until soft and porridge-like, then flavoured with tamarind, jaggery, and a warm, aromatic spice powder. The name simply means hot lentil rice, and it should be served steaming hot, loose in consistency, with a spoonful of ghee melting over the top. The taste is a balance of tangy, mildly sweet, savoury, and gently spiced, with the vegetables soft but still recognisable. It makes a complete lunch on its own and is a good way to use small quantities of mixed vegetables. One practical tip: keep the finished dish slightly runnier than you want it, because it thickens noticeably as it stands.

60 minutes
