Chicken Chettinad

4.8/5 (0 reviews)
Jump to Recipe
Share: WhatsApp Facebook X Pinterest

Chicken Chettinad is a bold chicken curry from the Chettinad region of Tamil Nadu, known for its freshly ground masala of roasted spices, black pepper, and coconut. The gravy is dark, thick, and deeply aromatic, with a peppery heat that builds slowly rather than hitting all at once. Grinding the masala fresh is what sets this curry apart from everyday chicken curries, and it takes only a few extra minutes. Serve it with steamed rice, dosa, appam, or rotis for a weekend lunch or a meal with guests. One practical tip: roast the spices on low heat only until they smell fragrant and darken slightly — if they burn, the whole curry turns bitter, so do not walk away during that step.

Step-by-Step Instructions

10 steps
  1. 1

    Step 1

    Wash the chicken pieces, drain them well, and keep aside.

  2. 2

    Step 2

    Make the Chettinad masala: heat a dry pan on low heat and roast the dried red chillies, coriander seeds, cumin seeds, fennel seeds, black peppercorns, cinnamon stick, green cardamoms, cloves, and star anise for 2 to 3 minutes until fragrant. Add the grated coconut and roast for 1 more minute until lightly golden. Cool completely, then grind to a fine paste with 3 to 4 tbsp of the measured water.

  3. 3

    Step 3

    Heat the sesame oil in a heavy kadhai on medium heat. Add the curry leaves and let them crackle.

  4. 4

    Step 4

    Add the sliced onions and cook for 6 to 7 minutes until deep golden at the edges.

  5. 5

    Step 5

    Add the ginger-garlic paste and cook for 1 minute until the raw smell goes.

  6. 6

    Step 6

    Add the chopped tomatoes and turmeric powder. Cook for 4 to 5 minutes until the tomatoes soften and the oil separates at the edges.

  7. 7

    Step 7

    Add the chicken pieces and salt. Cook on medium-high heat for 5 to 6 minutes, stirring now and then, until the chicken is sealed and lightly coloured.

  8. 8

    Step 8

    Add the ground Chettinad masala paste and mix well so the chicken is fully coated. Cook for 2 to 3 minutes.

  9. 9

    Step 9

    Pour in the remaining water, cover, and simmer on low heat for 18 to 20 minutes until the chicken is fully cooked and the gravy is thick and dark.

  10. 10

    Step 10

    Rest for 5 minutes off the heat, garnish with the chopped coriander leaves, and serve hot.

💡

Chef's Pro Tip

For optimal flavor, ensure all fresh spices are freshly roasted or ground before adding. Adjust seasoning gradually according to taste and let the dish rest for 3–5 minutes before serving.

North Indian

Paneer Tikka

Paneer Tikka is a popular North Indian starter of paneer cubes marinated in spiced hung curd and cooked until the edges char lightly and the centre stays soft. This is a home version made in an oven, on a grill, or on a tawa — a clay tandoor is not required, and the marinade does most of the work. The taste is smoky, tangy, and warmly spiced, with sweet bites of capsicum and onion between the paneer. Serve it hot as a starter or snack, or tuck it into wraps and rolls. The marination takes about 30 minutes and is inactive time, so it is not counted in the prep time below. One practical tip: use hung or very thick curd and pat the paneer dry first — a watery marinade slides off and the tikka steams instead of charring.

Kashmiri

Kashmiri Dum Aloo

Kashmiri Dum Aloo is a rich potato curry where small whole potatoes are fried until golden and then simmered slowly in a curd-based gravy scented with fennel, dry ginger, and Kashmiri red chillies. The gravy is deep red, mildly hot, and aromatic rather than fiery, and the potatoes soak up the flavour as they cook on dum. Traditionally, this version is made without onion and garlic, which keeps the taste clean and lets the whole spices come through. Serve it with steamed rice, naan, or rotis for a festive lunch or a special weekend meal. One practical tip: whisk the curd well and add it on low heat, stirring continuously for the first minute — this stops the gravy from splitting.

55 minutes

North Indian

Matar Pulao

Matar Pulao is a gentle, fragrant one-pot rice dish of basmati cooked with green peas and whole spices in ghee. The grains stay separate and fluffy, the peas add small bursts of sweetness, and the flavour is mild enough to sit next to almost any dal, curry, or raita. It is a useful everyday recipe — quick enough for a weekday lunch box and good enough to serve when guests come. You can make it in a pressure cooker or a heavy pot with a tight lid; the method below uses a pot so the texture is easy to control. One practical tip: rest the cooked pulao for 10 minutes before opening the lid and fluffing — this short rest is what keeps the grains from breaking and turning sticky.

30 minutes
