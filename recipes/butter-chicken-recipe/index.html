Butter Chicken

4.8/5 (0 reviews)
Jump to Recipe
Share: WhatsApp Facebook X Pinterest

Butter Chicken is a rich North Indian curry of tender chicken pieces simmered in a smooth tomato, butter and cream gravy. The sauce is mildly spiced, slightly sweet and silky, with a gentle smokiness from kasuri methi added at the end. It is most often linked to Delhi restaurant kitchens, though its exact origin story is often debated. Serve it hot with naan, roti or jeera rice for a family dinner or a small celebration meal. A practical tip: puree and strain the tomato-onion mixture before adding the chicken — that one step is what gives the gravy its restaurant-style smoothness without needing a lot of extra cream.

Step-by-Step Instructions

8 steps
  1. 1

    Step 1

    Mix the chicken with the curd, ginger-garlic paste, Kashmiri red chilli powder, turmeric powder, half the garam masala and half the salt. Cover and set aside while you start the gravy.

  2. 2

    Step 2

    Heat the oil and 1 tbsp of the butter in a kadhai on medium heat. Add the onion and cook for 5–6 minutes until soft and light golden.

  3. 3

    Step 3

    Add the tomatoes, cashew nuts and the remaining salt. Cook for 8–10 minutes, stirring often, until the tomatoes are completely soft and the oil starts to separate.

  4. 4

    Step 4

    Cool the mixture slightly, blend it until very smooth, then strain it back into the kadhai for a silky gravy.

  5. 5

    Step 5

    In a separate pan on medium-high heat, cook the marinated chicken pieces for 6–7 minutes, turning once, until lightly charred at the edges and almost cooked through.

  6. 6

    Step 6

    Add the chicken to the strained gravy along with the sugar and the remaining garam masala. Simmer on low heat for 5 minutes.

  7. 7

    Step 7

    Stir in the remaining 1 tbsp butter and the fresh cream. Rub the kasuri methi between your palms and sprinkle it in.

  8. 8

    Step 8

    Simmer for 1 more minute, switch off the heat and rest for 5 minutes before serving hot.

💡

Chef's Pro Tip

For optimal flavor, ensure all fresh spices are freshly roasted or ground before adding. Adjust seasoning gradually according to taste and let the dish rest for 3–5 minutes before serving.

North Indian

Baingan Bharta

Baingan Bharta is a smoky, mashed brinjal sabzi made by roasting a large brinjal directly over a flame until the skin chars and the flesh turns soft and fragrant, then cooking it with onion, tomato, ginger, garlic and everyday spices. The texture is soft and slightly chunky, not smooth like a puree, and the flavour is earthy with a gentle heat from green chillies. It is a comforting dinner dish that pairs beautifully with hot rotis, phulkas or even plain dal and rice. Serve it fresh and hot, because it loses its smoky punch on reheating. A practical tip: roast the brinjal until it completely collapses and feels heavy and soft — under-roasted brinjal stays spongy and never mashes well.

Indo-Chinese

Gobi Manchurian

Gobi Manchurian is an Indo-Chinese favourite of crisp fried cauliflower florets tossed in a glossy, garlicky soy-based sauce. The florets should stay crunchy under the sauce, with spring onion greens scattered on top for freshness. It works as a party starter on its own, or as a side with fried rice or plain noodles for a weekend dinner at home. The sauce is savoury, mildly sweet and just spicy enough to keep you reaching for another piece. A practical tip: toss the fried florets in the sauce only at the very end and serve straight away — even five minutes of sitting in the sauce will soften the coating you worked to make crisp.

45 minutes

Gujarati

Gujarati Kadhi

Gujarati Kadhi is a thin, pale-yellow curry made from curd and besan, gently sweetened and finished with a ghee tempering of mustard seeds, cumin, curry leaves and dry red chillies. Unlike thicker, pakoda-filled kadhis from some other regions, this version is light and pourable, with a clear sweet-sour balance. It is usually served poured over steamed rice, often as part of a simple Gujarati thali at home. The texture should be smooth, never grainy or split. A practical tip: whisk the curd and besan until completely lump-free before the pan goes on the heat, and keep stirring in one direction while it comes to a boil — that is what keeps the kadhi from curdling.

30 minutes
