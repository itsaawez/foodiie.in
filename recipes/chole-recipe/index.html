Chole (Kabuli Chana Curry)

4.8/5 (0 reviews)
Jump to Recipe
Share: WhatsApp Facebook X Pinterest

Chole is a Punjabi gravy curry of kabuli chana (chickpeas) simmered in a thick, tangy onion-tomato masala with ginger, green chillies and warming spices. The gravy should coat the chickpeas rather than drown them, and the chickpeas themselves should be soft enough to mash easily between two fingers. It is a favourite for weekend lunches and family dinners, served with bhature, pooris, jeera rice or plain rotis, along with sliced onion and a lemon wedge. The dish is planned ahead because the chickpeas need an overnight soak. A practical tip: reserve the chickpea cooking water and use it for the gravy — it adds body and a deeper colour that plain water cannot give.

Step-by-Step Instructions

11 steps
  1. 1

    Step 1

    Rinse the kabuli chana, soak it in enough water to cover generously for 8 hours or overnight, then drain.

  2. 2

    Step 2

    Add the soaked chickpeas, 4 cups fresh water and 1 tsp salt to a pressure cooker. Cook on medium heat for 5 to 6 whistles until the chickpeas are soft and mash easily. Reserve the cooking water; do not discard it.

  3. 3

    Step 3

    Heat the oil in a heavy kadai on medium heat. Add the cumin seeds and let them crackle.

  4. 4

    Step 4

    Add the chopped onions and cook for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring often, until deep golden brown. This browning builds the colour of the gravy, so do not rush it.

  5. 5

    Step 5

    Add the ginger-garlic paste and slit green chillies. Cook for 1 to 2 minutes until the raw smell goes away.

  6. 6

    Step 6

    Add the tomato puree, turmeric powder, red chilli powder and coriander powder. Cook on medium heat for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring often, until the masala thickens and oil separates at the sides.

  7. 7

    Step 7

    Add the chole masala powder and mix for 30 seconds.

  8. 8

    Step 8

    Add the cooked chickpeas along with about 2 cups of their reserved cooking water. Mix well, lightly mashing a few chickpeas against the side of the kadai to thicken the gravy.

  9. 9

    Step 9

    Simmer on low heat for 12 to 15 minutes until the gravy coats the chickpeas well. Adjust salt to taste and add a little more reserved cooking water if the gravy becomes too thick.

  10. 10

    Step 10

    Stir in the amchur and garam masala and simmer for 2 more minutes. Switch off the heat.

  11. 11

    Step 11

    Garnish with ginger strips and chopped coriander leaves. Serve hot with lemon wedges on the side.

💡

Chef's Pro Tip

For optimal flavor, ensure all fresh spices are freshly roasted or ground before adding. Adjust seasoning gradually according to taste and let the dish rest for 3–5 minutes before serving.

Punjabi

Aloo Paratha

Aloo Paratha is a Punjabi whole wheat paratha stuffed with a spiced mashed potato filling, rolled out gently and roasted on a tawa with ghee until golden spots appear on both sides. The outside should be crisp at the edges and soft inside, with the potato filling spread evenly right up to the edges — not collected in the centre. It is a much-loved breakfast, though it works just as well for lunch or a travelling meal, served with curd, pickle or a knob of butter. A practical tip: the potato filling must be completely cool and dry before stuffing; warm or wet filling makes the dough tear while rolling and leak on the tawa.

Hyderabadi

Vegetable Biryani

Vegetable Biryani is a layered rice dish where fragrant basmati rice is cooked over a spiced vegetable masala made with onion, tomato, curd and whole spices, then sealed and steamed on dum so the layers absorb each other's aroma. The rice should stay separate and fluffy, the vegetables tender but not mushy, and every serving should have both white and masala-coated grains. It suits festive lunches, Sunday family meals and dinner guests, served with raita or a simple salad. A practical tip: cook the rice only until it is about 70 percent done before layering — it finishes cooking in the dum steam, and fully cooked rice turns soft and broken by the end.

North Indian

Dal Tadka

Dal Tadka is a homestyle yellow dal made from arhar (toor) dal pressure-cooked until soft, simmered with a simple onion-tomato base, and finished with a sizzling tadka of ghee, garlic, cumin and dried red chillies poured over the top just before serving. The dal itself is smooth and mildly spiced, while the tadka adds the aroma and flavour that make the dish special. It is an everyday lunch or dinner staple served with steamed rice or rotis, and a little extra ghee on top is very welcome. A practical tip: add the tadka at the very end and cover the dal for 2 minutes — the dal absorbs the smoky garlic flavour much better than if you stir and serve immediately.

35 minutes
