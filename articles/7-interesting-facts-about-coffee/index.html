7 Interesting Facts About Coffee

By Aawez · Published 2 Sep 2026 · Updated 30 Sep 2026 · 3 min read

Coffee is the world's most popular pick-me-up, yet most of us drink it on autopilot. Behind that morning cup is a strange and fascinating history. Here are seven facts that might change how you see your next brew.

1. Coffee is a fruit

Those "coffee beans" are not beans at all — they are the seeds of a cherry-like fruit that grows on the coffee plant. In some places the sweet fruit pulp is dried and brewed as cascara, a tea-like drink. You have been drinking fruit seeds your whole life.

2. A shot of espresso has less caffeine than a mug of filter coffee

Espresso tastes stronger, so people assume it packs more caffeine. Per ounce it does — but a standard serving is tiny. A full mug of filter coffee (around 240 ml) typically contains more total caffeine than a single espresso shot. Size matters more than intensity.

3. Decaf is not caffeine-free

Decaffeination removes most — not all — of the caffeine. A cup of decaf still contains a small amount, roughly 2–5 mg compared to about 95 mg in regular coffee. If you are genuinely sensitive to caffeine, decaf is "less", not "none".

4. The "coffee nap" is real

Drinking coffee and then napping for 15–20 minutes can leave you more alert than either alone. Caffeine takes about 20 minutes to kick in, so you wake up just as it starts working — while the nap clears the sleepiness caffeine cannot fix. It sounds backwards, but the science holds up.

5. Finland drinks the most coffee on Earth

The average Finn drinks around four cups a day — more per person than anywhere else in the world. Coffee breaks are even written into many Finnish labour agreements. Italy may have the romance, but Finland has the volume.

6. Coffee was first eaten, not drunk

Long before brewing, people in coffee-growing regions mixed coffee cherries with animal fat into energy balls — an early energy bar. The drink we know came centuries later, and it took even longer to reach Europe.

7. Light roast has slightly more caffeine than dark roast

Roasting burns off a tiny bit of caffeine, so lighter roasts retain marginally more — at least when measured by scoop. (By weight the difference nearly vanishes, because dark-roast beans are lighter.) Either way, roast level changes flavour far more than kick.

"Coffee is a language in itself." — and like any language, it rewards the curious. The more you know about the cup, the better it tastes.

Try this: next time you brew, taste your coffee black before adding milk or sugar. You will notice flavours — chocolate, fruit, nuts — that were hiding behind the additives all along.

Which fact surprised you most? Coffee rewards curiosity, so keep experimenting with brews, roasts and methods. Your perfect cup is out there.
